Featured study guide

The spacing effect: why shorter, repeated sessions beat cramming

Memory research has repeatedly found that revising material in several short sessions spread over days or weeks leads to better long-term recall than the same total time spent in one block. Each time you return to a topic, retrieving it takes a little effort, and that effort strengthens the memory.

A simple way to apply it:

  1. Learn the topic and write a short set of questions about it.
  2. Test yourself the next day without looking at your notes.
  3. Test again after three days, then a week, then two weeks.
  4. Spend the most time on the questions you got wrong.

Study skills

Small habits that make revision more effective.

Test yourself

Closing the book and recalling a topic from memory works better than re-reading it.

Mix topics

Alternating between related topics in one session builds the skill of choosing the right method.

Plan the week

Give each subject a regular slot so revision becomes a routine rather than a decision.

Rest properly

Sleep and short breaks consolidate what you have learned. Protect both during exam season.
