Study guide of the month

The spacing effect: why shorter, repeated sessions beat cramming

Memory research has found again and again that revising something in several short sessions spread over days or weeks works better for long-term recall than spending the same amount of time on it in one go. Each time you come back to a topic, remembering it takes a bit of effort, and that effort is what makes the memory stronger.

An easy way to use it:

  1. Learn the topic and write a few questions about it.
  2. Test yourself the next day without looking at your notes.
  3. Test again after three days, then a week, then two weeks.
  4. Spend most of your time on the questions you got wrong.

Study skills

A few small habits that make revision work better:

  • Test yourself. Closing the book and recalling a topic from memory works better than re-reading it.
  • Mix topics. Switching between related topics in one session helps you get better at picking the right method.
  • Plan the week. Give each subject a regular slot so revision becomes a routine, not something you have to decide to do.
  • Rest properly. Sleep and short breaks help what you've learned stick. Protect both during exam season.
